
# Literature note

Hernández and Leblebicioğlu (2012) ask why pass-through has fallen over time. They use 35 car models imported into the US between 1980 and 2004 and let exporters switch between a high and a low pass-through pricing policy. Monetary stability explains more than half of the decline, ahead of market share and market concentration. Their paper is why I checked whether my estimate was stable across periods, and why my next step is to compare low- and high-inflation years. They use car-level data and a formal regime-switching model, while I use aggregate indexes and a simple before-and-after split.

Chen and Juvenal (2016) ask whether pass-through depends on product quality, using Argentine wine exports from 2002 to 2009. Higher-quality wines show more pricing-to-market, which they link to distribution costs paid in the destination currency. I use this as the explanation for exporters adjusting markups. They also note that aggregated data can bias pass-through estimates, which applies to my import price index.

Marazzi et al. (2005) document a sustained decline in pass-through to US import prices, from above 0.5 in the 1980s to around 0.2 in the decade to 2005. They find the largest estimates when commodity prices are excluded from the regression, and they point to a smaller share of commodity-intensive imports and more Chinese exporters as possible reasons. This relates to my oil control and my ex-petroleum comparison.
